Long prompts are not automatically good prompts. The useful detail is usually the decision you need to make and the evidence the answer should use.

## State what changes after the answer

Instead of asking for “a comprehensive analysis,” explain what you will choose or do next. Add the constraints that would make an otherwise plausible answer wrong.

For example: “Compare these two backup approaches for a single Mac. I care about reliable restores and low maintenance more than the lowest monthly price.”

## Make uncertainty visible

Ask the tool to separate what it can verify from what it is inferring. That makes the output easier to review and much safer to act on.
